1. **Spot market** — opens "Let's say you are an inference company… you need to reserve 1,024
   GPUs for three years." Peak vs. off-peak, "on average you only use 50% of the compute hours
   you reserve," relist the idle part on Hyperbolic's interruptible spot market, closes "you
   actually save half of your dollars, half of your money." Concrete scenario plus a number.
2. **Agents as customers** — "Right now we're currently serving humans… our fundamental belief is
   that agents are going to be the main consumers of compute." Agents won't "wine and dine with
   20, 30 neocloud sales," won't sign three-year contracts; they need a programmable way to see
   and provision all compute globally. Thesis clip.
3. **Long and short** — "Originally we thought we can just be the open marketplace model, we
   don't need to own any assets…" The market tightened, they were forced to reserve compute,
   so "we are basically trying to long on the physical side and leverage Silicon Data and other
   indices to short on the futures side." Story with a payoff (and a Silicon Data mention).
4. **IRR** — "Fundamentally we are the multi-cloud aggregation layer… for infrastructure, it's not
   just about ARR, it's about IRR." Selling on-demand instead of $4 offtake contracts for
   B200/B300 lifts revenue "even by just one or two dollars" and "their IRR is going to change
   from 30% to 100% plus." Number close.
5. **Market making** — Steven: "Do you also anticipate yourself entering, providing tokens and
   selling tokens?" Jasper: every inference player reserves long-term compute, breaks it out
   short-term for the liquidity premium, the open-source moat shrinks, "and in the end everyone
   is just trying to do market making on compute." Host question as cold open (kept in).
6. **Compute grid** — Steven: "You are basically a compute platform… someone is able to actually
   buy and sell compute." Jasper: "We wanted to build the first global compute grid… take compute
   elastically, but also sell it back just like electricity," plus the orchestration layer that
   moves a workload whenever it finds a better price.
