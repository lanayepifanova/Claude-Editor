Why is AI inference being split across two different chips? Carmen Li, CEO of Silicon Data and Compute Exchange, talks with a guest from Lightning AI, who previously worked at Cisco and in high-frequency trading, about what electronic trading and AI infrastructure have in common, and why inference is moving to separate prefill and decode hardware.

Silicon Data is the independent referee of the compute market. It publishes GPU price indices and benchmarks and holds no positions of its own, so it can measure supply and demand without bias. Its indices are the basis for compute futures with CME Group.

Compute Exchange is a neutral marketplace for buying and selling physical compute. It works like a NASDAQ for GPUs, connecting buyers with more than 150 providers worldwide. It now handles over $1 billion in quote requests every month, plus secondhand GPU sales.

Lightning AI, which has combined with Voltage Park, provides AI as a service: GPU infrastructure plus the software layer for research, training and inference, offered on demand or reserved.

In this conversation:
• The parallels between high-frequency trading, traditional HPC and AI workloads
• What separates one neocloud from another beyond having the latest GPUs
• Bare metal, virtualized or managed: how customers actually buy compute
• Disaggregated inference: running prefill and decode on different chips
• Why enterprises may hand training and fine-tuning to neoclouds, and the "coalition of experts" idea
