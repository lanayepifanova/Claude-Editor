YouTube title: Can GPU Prices Be Manipulated? Inside the Index Behind GPU Futures

YouTube description:

Can anyone manipulate the price of a GPU? Carmen Li, CEO of Silicon Data and Compute Exchange, sits down with Phoebe Liu, who covers AI infrastructure and semiconductors at The Information, to explain where GPU futures stand in regulatory review, and how an index is built so that no single provider can move the price.

Silicon Data is the independent referee of the compute market. It publishes GPU price indices and benchmarks and holds no positions of its own, so it can measure supply and demand without bias. Its indices are the basis for compute futures with CME Group.

Compute Exchange is a neutral marketplace for buying and selling physical compute. It works like a NASDAQ for GPUs, connecting buyers with more than 150 providers worldwide. It now handles over $1 billion in quote requests every month, plus secondhand GPU sales.

In this conversation:
• Where compute futures with CME Group stand in review with the CFTC and other regulators
• How the neocloud GPU index is built: no hyperscaler data, and no provider above 3.5%
• How provider data is anonymized, and why anomalous trades get thrown out
• Why an index provider can't be the referee and the player at the same time
• Why Nvidia benefits from futures, and what the market may look like in 12 months
