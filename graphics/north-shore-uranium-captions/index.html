The United States uses
about 50 million pounds
of uranium a year
to fuel its nuclear
reactors, and it mines
about 2 million.
The other 96%
is imported,
and for decades much
of it came from Russia,
Kazakhstan,
and Uzbekistan.
It wasn't always
like that.
The Grants Uranium
District in New Mexico
has produced over 340
million pounds
of uranium.
In the 1960s and 70s,
the mining company
Kerr-McGee drilled
more than 1,100 holes
into one part
of it called Rio Puerco.
Then the uranium price
collapsed,
and the ground sat idle
for decades.
Now the demand is coming
back.
More than 20 countries
have pledged to triple
nuclear capacity by
2050, and Microsoft,
Google, Amazon, and Meta
are all pursuing nuclear
power for their AI
data centres.
The uranium price
has roughly tripled
since 2020 to around 85
US dollars a pound,
and the US government
has named uranium
a critical mineral.
North Shore Uranium
is a small exploration
company that has gone
back to Rio Puerco.
It trades as NSU
on the TSX Venture
Exchange and NSURF
in the US, and it holds
an option to earn up
to 87.5% of the project.
In August 2026,
it finished its first
drill program there,
with 13 holes placed
next to the old
Kerr-McGee holes.
On September 3rd,
it reported uranium
mineralization in all
13 holes.
The best intercepts were
27 feet at 0.3%
eU3O8 and 12.5 feet
at 0.33%
eU3O8.
The E means the grade
was estimated
from radiation readings
taken down the hole,
and it has not yet been
confirmed by lab assays.
All 21 intercepts were
below the water table,
which matters
because of a mining
method called in-situ
recovery.
In-situ recovery
pumps a solution
through sandstone
to dissolve the uranium
and bring
it to the surface,
so there is no open pit
and no underground mine.
It is the main way
uranium is produced
in the US, and it only
works on uranium
that sits in sandstone
below the water table.
The geology at Rio
Puerco appears
favourable
for it, but it still
has to be confirmed by
more testing.
The chief executive,
Brooke Clements,
is a geologist
with more than 40 years
of experience.
He was a senior
executive at Peregrine
Metals, which was sold
for 487 million US
dollars.
The chief financial
officer and one
of the directors came
from Azarga Uranium,
which was bought by

The company also holds
two exploration
properties
in Saskatchewan's
Athabasca Basin, called
Falcon and West Bear.
