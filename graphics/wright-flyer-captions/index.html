This is the famous Wright Flyer.
It was built by Wilbur and Orville Wright,
two famous brothers who ran a bike
shop in Ohio.
They read everything that Lilienthal wrote
and they built gliders using his tables
of how much lift a wing makes.
But in 1901, their glider made a lot less
lift than his numbers said it should.
So they built a small wind tunnel
in their bike shop and they tested around
200 different wing shapes to get numbers
that they could trust.
Their biggest idea was about steering.
So a plane in the air can rotate three
different ways and the Wright brothers
realised the pilot needs a separate
control for each one.
This is called the three-axis control
and every plane since then has used it.
The first control is for roll where one
wing drops and the other rises.
Lilienthal rolled his glider by shifting
his body but the Wrights used cables
to twist the wingtips in opposite
directions which is called wing warping
and that is how the plane backed
into a turn.
The second control is for pitch
where the nose tips up or down
and for that they put a small wing
at the front called the elevator.
The third control is for yaw
where the nose swings left or right
and for that they added
a rudder at the back.
And with a control for each of the three,
the pilot could hold the plane level
and turn it on purpose.
They also needed an engine and so no
manufacturer would build one engine
that was light enough so their mechanic
Charlie Taylor built it himself
in about six weeks.
It made around 12 horsepower and weighed
around 80 kilograms partly because he cast
the main body in aluminium
which was unusual back then.
They had to also design their own
propellers because nobody had worked out
what shape a propeller for
a plane should be.
And the Wrights realised that a propeller
blade is an aerofoil which is the same
curved shape as a wing
so they could design the blades
with the wing data from their own
wind tunnel.
They put two propellers behind the wings
spinning in opposite directions
so that their twisting forces cancelled
out and the plane didn't roll to one side.
And on December 17, 1903 at Kitty Hawk
in North Carolina, the first flight lasted
12 seconds and it went about 37 metres,
just shorter than the wingspan of a modern
airliner but it was the first time
that anyone had flown a plane
with an engine and actually controlled it.
They flew three more times that day
and on the last flight they stayed up
for 59 seconds and covered about
260 metres.
The layout was pretty interesting too
so having the elevator at the front
is called a canard layout and the wings
drooped down slightly at the tip.
So both choices made the plane pretty
unstable and it was very hard to fly.
It wobbled a lot and the pilot had to keep
correcting it the whole time so pretty
much only the Wright
brothers could handle it.
